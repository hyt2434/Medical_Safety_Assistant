\section{Positioning}

\subsection{Problem Statement}

Patients may use several medicines at the same time, receive prescriptions from
different healthcare professionals, or know medicines only by local brand
names. Prescription text may be difficult to enter, and users may not know
which active ingredients are involved, whether the available data covers all
their medicines, or when a possible interaction needs professional attention.
Older adults are a primary user group because they are more likely to manage
multiple medicines while also experiencing limited vision, difficulty typing,
or unfamiliarity with smartphone applications and online medication resources.
These barriers can make it difficult to read medicine labels, search for
reliable information, enter prescription details accurately, and understand
complex safety warnings without assistance. Missed doses and unclear follow-up
further complicate safe medication use for both older adults and their family
caregivers.

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|L{0.27\textwidth}|Y|}
\hline
\textbf{The problem of} & Difficulty capturing, identifying, understanding, and consistently following multiple medicines while recognizing potential medication-safety risks and assessment limitations.\\ \hline
\textbf{Affects} & Primarily older adults, especially those using multiple medicines or having limited vision, digital confidence, or health literacy; family caregivers who assist them; other medicine users; and healthcare professionals who review medication-related questions.\\ \hline
\textbf{The impact of which is} & Users may overlook a potential interaction, misunderstand incomplete results, duplicate an active ingredient, miss a dose, or delay seeking qualified advice. Healthcare professionals may receive incomplete or unstructured information.\\ \hline
\textbf{A successful solution would} & Provide an accessible, plain-language experience for older adults; reduce typing through image and voice input while retaining manual alternatives; require correction and confirmation; collect the safety context needed for assessment; identify Vietnamese products and active ingredients; distinguish routine, review-required, and emergency outcomes without equating unknown with safe; support professional review; and optionally help users follow confirmed schedules.\\ \hline
\end{tabularx}

\subsection{Product Position Statement}

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|L{0.27\textwidth}|Y|}
\hline
\textbf{For} & Vietnamese patients, general medicine users, and family caregivers, with a supporting review view for healthcare professionals.\\ \hline
\textbf{Who} & Need a practical way to capture medicines, understand supported safety findings and limitations, organize confirmed schedules, and know when professional review is appropriate.\\ \hline
\textbf{The Medication Safety Assistant} & Is a responsive Web-based medication understanding and safety-support application.\\ \hline
\textbf{That} & Combines image, manual, typed, and voice input with user verification, Vietnamese medicine identification, supported interaction checks, understandable guidance, and medication follow-up.\\ \hline
\textbf{Unlike} & General search engines or chatbots, isolated interaction checkers, and reminder-only applications that address only part of the workflow or may not preserve uncertainty and local product identity.\\ \hline
\textbf{Our product} & Uses DAV data for Vietnamese medicine identification, maps verified active ingredients to DDInter 2.0 for supported interaction checking, keeps AI explanations subordinate to structured findings, immediately escalates emergency red flags, and never treats missing, unresolved, or unreviewed data as proof of safety.\\ \hline
\end{tabularx}
